The SCIRE (Social and Computational Intelligence Research) Group develops
computational methods for understanding language beyond its literal meaning
&mdash; argumentation, misinformation, narrative maneuvers, and regulatory
compliance. Before reaching out, browse the [research themes](/research/) and
[recent publications](/publications/) to see if there is a natural connection
to your own interests.

The principal investigator, Dr. Banerjee, can be reached at
[rbanerjee@cs.stonybrook.edu](mailto:rbanerjee@cs.stonybrook.edu).

---

> #### <span style="color: #a61919">Collaborators</span>
>
> Collaboration works best when it starts from a specific research question
> rather than a general interest in working together. If your work touches
> on misinformation, pragmatic language understanding, privacy and regulation,
> or biomedical language processing &mdash; or if you work in political science,
> sociology, public health, or law, and see a connection &mdash; please reach out
> directly with a brief description of what you have in mind.
{: .block-collaborators-contact}

> #### <span style="color: #2698ba">Postdoctoral Researchers</span>
> 
> The research group may occasionally host postdoctoral researchers on specific
> funded projects. Send an email with your academic CV, a publication list, a brief
> research statement (no more than 2 pages), and links to your homepage and any
> public code repositories.
{: .block-postdoc-contact}

> #### <span style="color: #EE9D3B">Prospective Ph.D. Students</span>
> 
> PhD students join through Stony Brook University's graduate programs. Apply to the
> [Ph.D. in Computer Science](https://www.cs.stonybrook.edu/students/Graduate-Studies/PhDProgram)
> or the [Ph.D. in Data Science](https://www.cs.stonybrook.edu/Admissions/Graduate-Admissions-Data-Science)
> program, and indicate in your application that you want to work with Dr. Banerjee.
>
> If you want to make contact before applying, your email should:
> - describe your background in NLP and/or ML concisely,
> - mention a specific paper or project from SCIRE and explain the connection
>   to your own interests, and
> - ask pose specific and well-formulated and convincing research question that you
>   would like to pursue as your primary topic.
>
> The group is highly selective and admits very few students. Strong candidates
> typically have a background in mathematics and statistics, solid Python programming
> skills (PyTorch experience is a strong plus), and ideally at least one prior research
> output.
>
> *Temporary or short-term research positions cannot be accommodated for candidates
> outside Stony Brook University.*
{: .block-prospectivephd-contant}

> #### Current Stony Brook Students
> 
> **Ph.D. students** already enrolled in Computer Science or Data Science
> are welcome to reach out about opportunities within the group. Ph.D. students are
> expected to have the ability to formulate a research problem that they want to pursue.
> Please be prepared to start your journey by presenting a convincing proposal for your
> research.
> 
> **M.S. (or 5-yr B.S./M.S.) students** (CSE 523/524 or thesis track CSE 599) should send:
> a brief statement of research interest, transcripts, a link to any code
> repositories, and prior research experience if any. Successful applicants
> will have strong ML/NLP coursework at the graduate level, solid Python
> Programming skills, and a commitment of approximately 10–12 focused hours per
> week. Work is expected to reach a caliber suitable for peer-reviewed publication.
> 
> **Undergraduate students** can participate through CSE 487 or the Honors
> Research sequence (CSE/HON/WSE 495/496). Exceptional performance in machine
> learning or NLP coursework is a prerequisite. The goal is publishable
> work; significant contributors are listed as co-authors.
> 
> *It may not always be possible to respond to individual queries.*
{: .block-current-contact}
